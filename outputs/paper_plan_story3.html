1Executive Summary

Some text-to-speech / voice-conversion systems are systematically hard for detectors — and that hardness is not random. We argue it has an objective geometric origin: the compactness of a system's speech trajectory in deep self-supervised (WavLM) space. We name this factor C and show it (i) predicts per-system hardness, (ii) is causal under direct intervention, and (iii) is actionable — targeting it lowers EER on the hardest systems.

Factor definitions

  • C (compactness) = −rog@L12 — negative radius of gyration of the WavLM layer-12 frame cloud. Higher C = more compact. Primary factor.
  • T (trajectory irregularity) = vel_entropy@L9 — entropy of frame-to-frame velocity at layer 9. Secondary (70/30), fragile.

One-line verdict on Story 1

The GAT-hierarchy interpretability story is mechanistically rich but narrow (ASVspoof-only, language-invariance untested). It belongs as a compressed supporting mechanism for Story 3 plus supplement — not a co-pillar, not a standalone paper.

What's strong (lead with these)

  • Hardness is system-intrinsic — systems agree on what's hard across seeds (reproducible ranking), so we are explaining signal, not noise.
  • C ↔ hardness on MLAAD (63 systems): r ≈ +0.35, LOO R² ≈ 0.069 for C; more compact → harder.
  • C is causal (E9): scaling compactness inside the frozen detector moves EER through baseline and beats a matched-energy shift control in both directions.
  • C transfers to in-the-wild at the speaker level (ρ(C, EER) = +0.44, n=45 speakers).
  • C is actionable: C/T-guided calibration (P1) and hardness reweighting (P5) reduce EER on the hardest (C-Q4) systems by ≈ 0.023 with AUC +0.026.

What's fragile (handle carefully)

  • T is fragile — scale-dependent, not independently manipulable in E9, and likely distorted in in-the-wild audio. Keep at 70/30; caveat, don't denounce.
  • Encoder scope — C validates on WavLM & wav2vec2 but fails on HuBERT. Never claim "all SSL."
  • Coverage — C+T explain ≈ 9% of residual-hardness variance (LOO). Frame as "actionable factors," not "we explain hardness."

⚠ Reframe required: the smoothing sub-story

The earlier narrative ("smoothing improved detection on hard systems") is not what the data shows, but it is also not a failure. Two distinct results:

  • Smoothing as training augmentation (P4) is a dose-dependent per-system lever. Applied uniformly the mean regresses (overall EER Δ=+0.037), but it genuinely helps a subset: 8 systems improve on both EER and AUC (15 on EER) — e.g. MegaTTS3 −0.134 EER / +0.078 AUC, Resemble.ai −0.078, FireRedTTS-2.0 −0.072 / +0.113. Reading: it overcorrects systems that don't need it and helps those that do ⇒ hardness is targetable (same logic as P5).
  • Smoothing as inference intervention establishes causality: removing temporal variance monotonically hurts detection (MA k=3→k=10: EER 0.421→0.571) ⇒ temporal smoothness is a hardness factor ⇒ this is what motivated T.

Do not write "smoothing uniformly improved detection," and do not write "smoothing augmentation failed."

3Story 3 as the Primary Framing

Thesis. Deepfake-detection hardness has an objective, measurable geometric origin in deep SSL space: synthetic-speech systems whose layer-12 WavLM trajectories are more compact (factor C) are systematically harder to detect; this factor is causal and can be acted on. A secondary dynamic factor (T, velocity-entropy) correlates with hardness but is fragile.

The evidence arc (paper spine)

  1. Objective — hardness rankings agree across seeds → not model noise.
  2. Geometric correlate — C predicts per-system hardness on MLAAD (63 systems); T adds a weaker, correlated signal.
  3. Independent — C ⟂ T (low correlation r≈+0.11; separate PCA components; positive unique partials).
  4. Causal — intervening on C inside the frozen detector moves EER beyond a matched-energy shift control (E9).
  5. Actionable — C/T-guided calibration (P1) and reweighting (P5) lower hardest-system EER.
  6. External validity — C transfers to in-the-wild at the speaker level; T treated cautiously there.

Dual-venue framing note

Speech (Interspeech / ICASSP): foreground the EER tables, the MLAAD / ITW benchmarks, and actionability (§8); position C/T as a diagnostic that improves anti-spoofing. ML / interp (NeurIPS / ICLR): foreground the causal-intervention design (E9) and the representation-geometry framing (§§6–7); position the detector as a probe into why synthetic-speech manifolds resist linear separation.

4Story 1 Honest Review

Evaluating the mechanistic-interpretability / GAT-hierarchy storyline on its own merits.

If Story 1 were the primary paper, what is its core contribution?

A hierarchical functional dissociation in a phoneme graph-attention detector: early message-passing layers linearly encode vocoder / system identity (multiclass probe 91.8% @ gat_l0 → 89.3% gat_l1 → 85.3% gat_l2 → 75.0% bilstm), while binary spoof detection strengthens with depth (AUC 0.95→0.98). Causally, activation patching shows a dual-subspace split: vowels/diphthongs drive the binary decision (Δ≈+1.95) whereas sibilants/nasals drive system-identity (probe shift Δ≈+0.004) — resolving a representational-vs-causal paradox (sibilants have the largest PC1 delta yet are causally weak for the binary task because the model already discounts them).

Is it strong enough for an entire publication by itself?

No (not yet). It is evaluated on the 6 ASVspoof 2019 LA systems (A01–A06), the multiclass causal shifts are tiny (±0.004) and boundary-sensitive, and the central assumption of language-invariant phoneme abstraction is untested and is in tension with the (separate) language-overfitting evidence.

Where is it strongest — standalone / secondary / mechanism-for-Story-3 / supplement?

Strongest as a mechanistic explanation supporting Story 3: it explains why compact, hard systems resist the linear separability the GAT exploits. Compress to one section + supplement.

13Final Recommendation
