(a) "DETECTORS FAIL TO TRANSFER ACROSS DOMAINS"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PAPER CLAIM: Abstract/Intro, lines 2-4, 22-24: "a detector trained on one
recording domain often fails on another"; Sec 4 line 94: "Why does a predictor
(or detector) fit on one corpus fail on another?" The paper asserts cross-domain
failure as accepted background but cites nothing at the point of claim.

WHY IT NEEDS SUPPORT: This is the central motivating premise. It is well
established in the literature and should be anchored so reviewers see the paper's
contribution (a geometric EXPLANATION) sits on top of a documented problem.

SOURCES:
  1. Nicolas M. Müller, Pavel Czempin, Franziska Dieckmann, Adam Froghyar,
     Konstantin Böttinger. "Does Audio Deepfake Detection Generalize?"
     Interspeech 2022, pp. 2783-2787. — The canonical demonstration that
     detectors trained on one corpus collapse on out-of-distribution ("in the
     wild") data; also introduces the In-the-Wild dataset.
  2. Nicolas M. Müller, Nicholas Evans, Hemlata Tak, Philip Sperl, Konstantin
     Böttinger. "Harder or Different? Understanding Generalization of Audio
     Deepfake Detection." arXiv:2406.03512, 2024 (Interspeech 2024). —
     Decomposes the in- vs out-of-domain gap into "hardness" and "difference";
     finds the gap is dominated by DIFFERENCE, directly relevant to the paper's
     "the axis rotates" explanation.
  3. Xin Wang, Junichi Yamagishi, et al. "ASVspoof 2021: Towards Spoofed and
     Deepfake Speech Detection in the Wild." arXiv:2210.02437, 2022
     (also IEEE/ACM TASLP). — Challenge summary documenting robustness/
     generalization degradation under new conditions.
SUGGESTED KEYS: muller2022wild (ALREADY IN .bib), muller2024harder (ADD),
  wang2022asvspoof21summary (ADD, optional).

(d) SSL LAYER-WISE ANALYSES JUSTIFYING LAYER-12 GEOMETRY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PAPER CLAIM: Sec 2 lines 59-66 & Fig 1 caption: geometry is read from "frozen
layer-12 space" / "WavLM-L12," with no justification for why a deep layer is the
right place to find natural-vs-synthetic linguistic structure.

WHY IT NEEDS SUPPORT: The choice of layer 12 (deepest, most linguistically
abstract) is defensible but currently unsupported. Layer-wise probing studies
show information stratifies from acoustic (early) to phonetic/word/semantic
(deep) — exactly the rationale. (Also directly relevant to roadmap X4's layer
sweep.)

SOURCES:
  1. Ankita Pasad, Ju-Chieh Chou, Karen Livescu. "Layer-Wise Analysis of a
     Self-Supervised Speech Representation Model." IEEE ASRU 2021
     (arXiv:2107.04734). — The standard reference that information is
     stratified across layers (acoustic early, linguistic deep) in wav2vec 2.0.
  2. Ankita Pasad, Chung-Ming Chien, Shane Settle, Karen Livescu. "What Do
     Self-Supervised Speech Models Know About Words?" Transactions of the ACL
     (TACL), 12:372-391, 2024. — Extends the layer-wise analysis (incl. HuBERT/
     WavLM-family) to word identity/boundary/semantics; supports "deep layers
     carry linguistic content."
  3. Sanyuan Chen, Chengyi Wang, et al. "WavLM: Large-Scale Self-Supervised
     Pre-Training for Full Stack Speech Processing." IEEE JSTSP 16(6):1505-1518,
     2022. — The WavLM paper's own layer-weighting (learned per-layer weights
     across downstream tasks) motivates using deep layers. (ALREADY IN .bib.)
SUGGESTED KEYS: pasad2021layerwise (ADD), pasad2024words (ADD),
  chen2022wavlm (ALREADY IN .bib).

(g) HIGH-DIMENSIONAL CHANCE COSINE CONCENTRATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PAPER CLAIM: Sec 4 lines 98-99 & Fig 2b: "against a 768-dimensional chance floor
of ~0.03"; cross-corpus cosines "inside the high-dimensional chance band." The
claim that random directions in 768-D concentrate near cosine 0 (~1/sqrt(d)) is
stated without a source.

WHY IT NEEDS SUPPORT: The entire "the axis rotates to near-orthogonality" result
depends on knowing that ~0.03 is the chance level; a reader should be pointed to
the concentration-of-measure fact.

SOURCES:
  1. Michel Ledoux. "The Concentration of Measure Phenomenon." Mathematical
     Surveys and Monographs vol. 89, American Mathematical Society, 2001. —
     Standard reference for concentration on the high-dimensional sphere (random
     unit vectors are nearly orthogonal).
  2. Roman Vershynin. "High-Dimensional Probability: An Introduction with
     Applications in Data Science." Cambridge University Press, 2018. —
     Modern textbook; explicit E|cos| ~ 1/sqrt(d) / quasi-orthogonality of random
     vectors (Ch. 3), directly the ~0.03 = 1/sqrt(768) claim.
  3. Persi Diaconis, David Freedman. "Asymptotics of Graphical Projection
     Pursuit." Annals of Statistics, 12(3):793-815, 1984. — Classic result that
     high-dimensional projections concentrate (near-orthogonality of random
     directions). (Optional, historical.)
SUGGESTED KEYS: vershynin2018hdp (ADD), ledoux2001concentration (ADD, optional).
